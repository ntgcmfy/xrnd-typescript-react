The research covers only these 3 points:
1. The main professional jobs (4)
2. The problems in each job (only those an AI API could help solve)
3. The documents people have to read or write

## 2. Problems in each job (only those an AI API could help solve)

### A. Writing job postings / job descriptions
- 65.2% say "we can't write postings that attract applicants", 53.8% say "we can't write copy that fits the target people", 27.3% say "we don't know what to write", 25.8% say "we want to use AI but don't know how".
- One posting takes about 30 minutes. Many staff write about 7 per month alone, on top of other duties.
- Postings look similar to competitors; it is hard to differentiate and put the company's appeal into words (76.5% of mid-career hiring staff reported problems).
- Since April 2024, more items must be stated (e.g. "scope of change"). There is a risk of missing items, exaggeration, and expressions implying age or gender, which break the law.
- AI idea: interview notes from the hiring department → a draft posting with all legal items; check for banned expressions and missing items.

- https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000256.000075909.html (Tenshoku Ichiba survey on writing job postings)
- https://media.bizreach.biz/18841 (BizReach: problems in writing job postings)

### B. Selection management, interview scheduling, interview records
- The hardest work for hiring staff: scheduling 48.7%, organizing applicant information 45.1%, communicating with applicants 35.5%.
- About 80% of companies take 1–5 days to schedule an interview (71.2% say the ideal is same day to next day). "Scheduling is complicated" is the top complaint (51.9%).
- Time per applicant (median): record after the meeting 21 min, scheduling 14 min, message back-and-forth 13 min, pass/fail and internal alignment 12 min, notice of result 10 min, document check 4 min.
- The main cause of stalled selection is "internal pass/fail decisions and condition adjustment" (40.2%). Evaluations differ by interviewer, so decisions take time.
- AI idea: interview notes/transcript → an evaluation sheet summarized per criterion; generate scheduling and result emails to applicants; summarize and organize applicant information.

### C. Offer to onboarding procedures
- Many documents are needed. Missing or late submissions delay insurance and payroll processing.
- Deadlines are strict: social insurance enrollment within 5 days of the join date, employment insurance by the 10th of the month after joining. Mistakes are likely with early-month joins or several joiners at once.
- Troubles include a changed join date not being reflected, poor handling of personal information such as My Number, and missing side-job declarations.
- Offer notices, working-conditions notices and acceptance letters are made per person; mismatched understanding of conditions leads to declined offers or early resignation.
- AI idea: from a candidate's conditions (employment type, location, salary, etc.), draft the offer notice, working-conditions notice and onboarding guide email, and check for missing legal items. Work backward from the join date to create tasks and guide text for each deadline.

### D. Performance reviews and meeting records
- Evaluators' most common worry is how to write review comments. They don't know what to write, so comments become abstract or safe ("worked hard").
- Review sheets are filled in during busy periods. Comments are sometimes left blank.
- Evaluation bias: focusing on end-of-term events, being swayed by impressions, rating everyone in the middle. The rating and the comment can contradict each other.
- Facts are not recorded during the term, so evidence-based comments are hard to write. Meeting records also need organizing.
- AI idea: the manager's bullet notes → a comment draft in the form "fact → evaluation → expectation"; check for personality judgments, comparisons with others, and contradictions with the rating; tidy meeting notes.

## 3. Documents people have to read or write

| Job | Documents written | Documents read |
|-----|-------------------|----------------|
| A Job posting | Job posting, job description, recruitment guidelines | Hiring plan, interview notes from the hiring department, competitors' postings, the items required by the Employment Security Act |
| B Selection | Interview scheduling emails, reminder emails, result notices, post-interview records and evaluation sheets, pass/fail reports to the company | Resumes, career histories, applicant information, evaluation criteria |
| C Onboarding | Offer notice, acceptance letter / pledge, working-conditions notice (employment contract), onboarding guide, the three legally required ledgers, social and employment insurance filings | Documents submitted by the candidate (pension number, employment insurance certificate, withholding slip, tax-deduction declaration, etc.), Labor Standards Act Article 15 |
| D Review | Review comments, meeting records (start/mid/end of term), development plan | Goal-setting sheet, self-evaluation, records during the term, evaluation criteria, previous review |
